Jane Austen (1775–1817) was an English novelist whose six novels of courtship and marriage examine the English landed gentry at the end of the eighteenth century. Four of them, *Sense and Sensibility*, *Pride and Prejudice*, *Mansfield Park* and *Emma*, appeared anonymously in her lifetime, and *Northanger Abbey* and *Persuasion* were published after her death. Her irony, wit and close attention to speech and social detail made her one of the founders of the realistic novel in English.

## Life

Jane Austen was born on 16 December 1775 at the rectory of Steventon, in Hampshire, where her father, the Reverend George Austen, was rector. Her mother was Cassandra Leigh, of the prominent Leigh family. She grew up in a large family of brothers, with one sister, Cassandra, who remained her closest friend for life.

In 1783 the two sisters were sent to Oxford to be taught by Ann Cawley, who took them to Southampton later that year; that autumn both girls caught typhus, and Jane nearly died. From early in 1785 they attended the Reading Abbey Girls' School, but the fees were too high, and they came home before the end of 1786. After that her education came from reading, guided by her father and brothers, with free use of her father's library. The family staged plays, mostly comedies, in the rectory barn. From about the age of eleven she wrote stories, poems and plays to amuse the household, and from 1787 to 1793 she copied twenty-nine of these early works into three notebooks, among them the satirical novel in letters *Love and Freindship* (1790) and a parody *History of England* (1791). The short novel in letters *Lady Susan* followed in the mid-1790s.

At the end of 1795 she flirted with Tom Lefroy, a young Irishman visiting the neighbourhood, but neither had money and his family sent him away in January 1796. Her first full-length novel, *Elinor and Marianne*, was read to the family before 1796. In 1796 she began *First Impressions*, finishing it in August 1797; in November her father offered it to the London publisher Thomas Cadell, who declined it by return of post. She then recast *Elinor and Marianne* as a third-person narrative close to *Sense and Sensibility*, and from 1798 wrote *Susan*, a satire on the Gothic novel that became *Northanger Abbey*. In 1803 the publishers Crosby & Company paid £10 for *Susan*, advertised it, and never brought it out.

In December 1800 her father decided to retire and move the family to Bath. There she began and abandoned *The Watsons*. In December 1802 she accepted a proposal of marriage from Harris Bigg-Wither, the heir to a large estate, and withdrew her acceptance the next morning. Her father died in Bath on 21 January 1805, leaving his wife and daughters dependent on contributions from the sons. For several years they lived in rented lodgings and with relatives, including a period in Southampton with her brother Frank. On 7 July 1809 they settled in a cottage at Chawton, in Hampshire, on the estate of her brother Edward.

At Chawton she revised her early manuscripts. Through her brother Henry, Thomas Egerton published *Sense and Sensibility* in October 1811, "By a Lady," at her own financial risk; it sold out and earned her £140. Egerton bought the copyright of *Pride and Prejudice* for £110 and published it in January 1813, and *Mansfield Park* followed in May 1814. Her name never appeared on her books in her lifetime. The Prince Regent admired her novels, and in November 1815 his librarian, James Stanier Clarke, invited her to visit the prince's London residence and hinted that she should dedicate her next novel to the prince. *Emma* was published by John Murray. While it was in the press she began *The Elliots*, later *Persuasion*, and finished it in August 1816.

By early 1816 she was unwell, and the failure of Henry's bank in March of that year cost several of her brothers heavily. In January 1817 she began a new novel, later published as *Sanditon*, and stopped work on it in March. In May Cassandra and Henry took her to Winchester for treatment, and she died there on 18 July 1817, aged 41. She was buried in the north aisle of the nave of Winchester Cathedral. In a "Biographical Notice" prefixed to the posthumous edition of *Northanger Abbey* and *Persuasion*, Henry Austen named his sister as the author of the novels for the first time.

## Writing

Austen worked within a narrow world of country families, villages, and resorts such as Bath, and she described her art, in her own phrase, as "the little bit (two inches wide) of ivory" on which she worked "with so fine a brush." Her plots turn on women's dependence on marriage for social standing and economic security. She rejected the sentimental and Gothic fiction popular in her youth, parodying it in her juvenilia and in *Northanger Abbey*, and returned to the tradition of Samuel Richardson and Henry Fielding for a comic and realistic study of manners. She was the first English novelist to make extensive use of free indirect speech, which lets a narrator give a character's thoughts in the character's own idiom while keeping an ironic distance.

Her books were fashionable in her lifetime but seldom reviewed. Walter Scott reviewed *Emma* anonymously and praised "the art of copying from nature as she really exists in the common walks of life"; Scott also wrote, "The big bow-wow I can do myself like any one going; but the exquisite touch which renders commonplace things and characters interesting from the truth of the description and the sentiment is denied to me." Macaulay thought *Mansfield Park* her greatest achievement. In 1833 Richard Bentley reissued the six novels in his Standard Novels series, and they have been in print ever since. Her nephew James Edward Austen-Leigh's *A Memoir of Jane Austen* (1869) brought a wider public, and Leslie Stephen called the enthusiasm of the 1880s "Austenolatry." Charlotte Brontë and Mark Twain were among her detractors. R. W. Chapman's edition of 1923 was the first scholarly edition of any English novelist.

## In this library

- [Sense and Sensibility](sense-and-sensibility) — After their father's death, the Dashwood sisters and their mother lose their home in Sussex and move to a cottage in Devon, where the two eldest, Elinor and Marianne, of contrasting characters, each experience love and heartbreak. Her first published novel, issued anonymously "By a Lady" by Thomas Egerton in 1811.
- [Pride and Prejudice](pride-and-prejudice) — Mr Bennet's estate is entailed away from his five daughters, and his second daughter, Elizabeth, learns the cost of her hasty first judgement of the proud and wealthy Mr Darcy. A revision of *First Impressions*, published by Thomas Egerton in 1813.
- [Mansfield Park](mansfield-park) — At ten Fanny Price is sent from her poor family in Portsmouth to live with her rich uncle Sir Thomas Bertram at Mansfield Park, where all her cousins except Edmund mistreat her. Published by Thomas Egerton in 1814, with a second edition from John Murray in 1816.
- [Emma](emma) — In the Surrey village of Highbury, the handsome, clever and rich Emma Woodhouse takes up matchmaking for her friend Harriet Smith, against the advice of Mr Knightley. Published by John Murray; its title page is dated 1816.
- [Northanger Abbey](northanger-abbey) — Catherine Morland, a young reader of Gothic novels, goes to Bath with her neighbours the Allens, meets Henry Tilney, and is invited to stay at his family's home, Northanger Abbey, which she expects to be as frightening as the houses in her novels. Written as *Susan* and sold to Crosby in 1803, it was published after her death together with *Persuasion*.
- [Persuasion](persuasion) — Anne Elliot, twenty-seven, was persuaded years before to break her engagement to Frederick Wentworth; when her family lets their estate to an admiral to reduce their debts and moves to Bath, Captain Wentworth, the admiral's brother-in-law, comes back into her life. Her last completed novel, published after her death together with *Northanger Abbey*.
