Voltaire (1694–1778), born François-Marie Arouet, was a French writer, philosopher, satirist and historian of the Enlightenment. Famous for his wit and his attacks on intolerance and on the Catholic Church, he wrote plays, poems, histories, essays and philosophical tales, of which *Candide* is the best known.

## Life

François-Marie Arouet was born in Paris on 21 November 1694 and baptized the next day, the youngest child of François Arouet, a notary who later held a minor treasury post, and Marie Marguerite Daumard. He was educated by the Jesuits at the Collège Louis-le-Grand. His father wanted him to become a lawyer, but he had already decided to be a writer, and his wit made him welcome in aristocratic society. In 1713 he went to The Hague as secretary to the French ambassador, but a love affair with a young Protestant refugee got him sent home.

Satirical verses attributed to him against the Regent led to his imprisonment in the Bastille in May 1717; he spent nearly a year there and came out in April 1718, after which he called himself Voltaire. His tragedy *Œdipe*, staged by the Comédie-Française in November 1718, was a great success, and his epic poem on Henri IV, later called *La Henriade*, was printed secretly and smuggled into Paris. In 1726 a quarrel with the nobleman Rohan-Chabot, whose servants beat him, ended with Voltaire in the Bastille again, and he was allowed to go into exile in England at his own request. During more than two years there he met Pope, Gay and Swift, read Shakespeare and studied Newton, and he admired English liberty and religious tolerance. His *Letters Concerning the English Nation* appeared in London in 1733; their French version, the *Lettres philosophiques* (1734), was burned and banned, and he had to leave Paris.

In 1733 Voltaire had begun a long relationship with Émilie du Châtelet, a gifted mathematician, and from 1734 they lived at her husband's château of Cirey, on the borders of Champagne and Lorraine. There they studied science, history and the Bible, and Voltaire's *Elements of the Philosophy of Newton* helped to win acceptance for Newton's theories in France. In 1736 Frederick, then crown prince of Prussia, began a correspondence with him. After Émilie's death in childbirth in 1749, Voltaire accepted Frederick's invitation and in 1750 moved to Potsdam as the king's chamberlain. There he finished *The Age of Louis XIV*, but his satire on Maupertuis, president of the Berlin Academy, angered the king, and after he left Prussia Frederick's agents detained him at Frankfurt for several weeks.

Barred from Paris, Voltaire bought the estate of Les Délices near Geneva in 1755, and at the end of 1758 the larger estate of Ferney, on French soil a few miles from Geneva, where he lived for most of his last twenty years as the most famous man of letters in Europe. *Candide* appeared early in 1759 and the *Dictionnaire philosophique* in 1764. Visitors such as James Boswell, Adam Smith, Casanova and Edward Gibbon came to Ferney. From 1762 he campaigned for victims of religious persecution, above all the Protestant merchant Jean Calas, who had been tortured to death on the charge of murdering his son, and he won the reversal of the verdict in 1765.

In February 1778, aged eighty-three, Voltaire returned to Paris for the first time in many years to see his tragedy *Irène*, and he was received as a hero. He fell ill soon after and died on 30 May 1778. Refused a Christian burial in Paris, he was buried quietly at the abbey of Scellières in Champagne. In 1791 the revolutionary National Assembly, honouring him as a forerunner of the Revolution, had his remains carried to the Panthéon in Paris.

## Writing

Voltaire worked in almost every literary form: tragedies, epic and comic verse, history, philosophy, polemic and fiction, as well as more than twenty thousand letters. He was one of the first authors to win wide commercial success across Europe, and he wrote at constant risk from the French censors, often publishing anonymously or denying works that were plainly his. His themes were religious tolerance, freedom of thought and the folly of dogma, and his weapons were clarity, speed and irony.

The 1911 *Britannica* called his prose tales "incomparably the most remarkable and most absolutely good fruit of his genius," and observed that they were usually written as pamphlets with a polemical aim: *Candide* attacks optimism, and *Zadig* and others attack received beliefs. In *Micromégas*, as in Montesquieu's *Persian Letters*, a visitor from outside looks at European customs and finds them absurd. *Candide*, his answer to Leibniz's doctrine that this is the best of all possible worlds and to the Lisbon earthquake of 1755, ends with the advice that "we must cultivate our garden." As a historian, in *The Age of Louis XIV* and other works, he set aside supernatural explanations and the narrative of wars and diplomacy for the study of customs, social life and achievements in the arts and sciences, and he had an enormous influence on the writing of history.

## In this library

- [Micromégas](micromegas) — Micromégas, a giant from a planet of the star Sirius, banished for a book about insects, travels through space with a smaller companion from Saturn, and the two reach the Earth, where with a magnifying lens made from a diamond they discover a ship of philosophers and are amazed at the pride of such tiny beings. It is generally dated to 1752, the year of an edition titled *Le Micromégas de M. de Voltaire* bearing the imprint "London."
- [Candide](candide) — Candide, a young man brought up in a Westphalian castle by the tutor Pangloss to believe that all is for the best in the best of all possible worlds, is driven out into a world of war, earthquake, the Inquisition, slavery and swindling, and learns at last to cultivate his garden. *Candide, ou l'Optimisme*, presented as "translated from the German of Doctor Ralph," was published secretly in January 1759 in several countries at once, with editions from Cramer in Geneva, Marc-Michel Rey in Amsterdam, Jean Nourse in London and Lambert in Paris, and it was soon banned.
