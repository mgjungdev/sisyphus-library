Mikhail Lermontov (1814–1841) was a Russian Romantic poet, novelist and dramatist, hailed in his lifetime as the heir of Pushkin. He is known for lyric and narrative poems such as "Death of the Poet," *The Demon* and *Mtsyri*, and for *A Hero of Our Time*, regarded as the first Russian psychological novel. He was killed in a duel at the age of twenty-six.

## Life

Mikhail Yuryevich Lermontov was born in Moscow in October 1814. His family traced its descent from the Scottish Learmonths through George Learmonth, an officer who settled in Russia in the seventeenth century. His father, Yuri Petrovich Lermontov, was an army captain; his mother, Maria Mikhaylovna Arsenyeva, died of tuberculosis when he was two. His grandmother Elizaveta Arsenyeva took custody of the boy and raised him on her estate of Tarkhany in Penza province, where he received an aristocratic home education in languages, music and painting. He was often ill as a child, and visits to the Caucasus in his early years left a lasting impression on him.

In 1827 he moved with his grandmother to Moscow. He entered the boarding school attached to Moscow University in 1829 and the university itself in 1830, where the poet Alexey Merzlyakov was among his teachers, and left in 1832. He then went to St Petersburg, attended the School of Cavalry Junkers and Ensigns of the Guard, and was commissioned in the Life Guard Hussars, stationed at Tsarskoye Selo. Alongside his duties he wrote the verse drama *Masquerade* (1835) and the narrative poems *The Tambov Treasurer's Wife* and *Boyarin Orsha*.

The death of Pushkin after a duel in 1837 made Lermontov famous. His poem "Death of the Poet," whose closing lines accused the court and high society of complicity in Pushkin's death, circulated widely; the authorities took it as an insult, Lermontov was arrested and exiled to a dragoon regiment in the Caucasus. He flourished there, studying local languages, painting, collecting folklore and travelling widely, and he came to know the Decembrist poet Alexander Odoyevsky and the Georgian circle of Alexander Chavchavadze. Through the efforts of his grandmother and of General Benckendorff he was transferred back, first to a cavalry regiment at Nizhny Novgorod and then to the Hussars of the Guard near the capital.

In St Petersburg he was received in the salons as Pushkin's successor. His historical poem "Borodino" appeared in *Sovremennik*, and with the help of Vasily Zhukovsky he published "The Song of Merchant Kalashnikov." Between 1839 and 1840 parts of *A Hero of Our Time* appeared in the journal *Otechestvennye Zapiski*, and in 1840 the novel came out as a book, followed in October 1840 by his first collection of poems.

Early in 1840 Lermontov fought a duel near the Chernaya River with Ernest de Barante, son of the French ambassador, and was slightly wounded and arrested. He was sent back to the Caucasus, where he joined General Galafeyev's detachment fighting the forces of Imam Shamil and distinguished himself in the battle on the Valerik River in July 1840, which he described in his poem "Valerik." In 1841 he was allowed a short leave in St Petersburg but was soon ordered back to the south. He reached Stavropol in May and went on to Pyatigorsk, where he lingered on grounds of illness. His relentless mockery of Nikolai Martynov, a former fellow cadet, led Martynov to challenge him, and in July 1841 the two fought at the foot of Mount Mashuk. Martynov's shot struck Lermontov in the heart and killed him instantly. He was buried in Pyatigorsk without military honours, and in 1842 the Tsar permitted his coffin to be moved to the family cemetery at Tarkhany.

## Writing

Lermontov's career as a published writer lasted only about six years, but it left some thirty longer poems, several hundred lyrics and five dramas. His early verse shows the influence of Byron, who remained his model throughout, and of Pushkin, with proud outcasts as its heroes; D. S. Mirsky called "The Angel" (1831) perhaps the finest Romantic verse ever written in Russian. His later poetry balanced Romanticism with realism in historical poems and nature lyrics, while poems such as "The Thought" (1838) voiced political disillusionment. The long poem *The Demon*, about a fallen angel, could not be published in Russia and first appeared in Berlin in 1856. His erotic cadet verses were published only in 1936. In *Mtsyri* (1839) a dying young monk tells of the few days of freedom he preferred to life in the monastery, and "The Song of Merchant Kalashnikov" was admired for its authentic use of the manner of Russian folk epic; his "Cossack Lullaby," drawn from folklore, passed back into living folk tradition.

The critic Skabichevsky described Lermontov's mood as a "Russian spleen," joining deep melancholy with a wild longing for freedom. *A Hero of Our Time* founded the Russian psychological novel. Its stories are arranged out of chronological order, so that Pechorin, a Byronic "superfluous man," is seen first from outside and only then through his own journal, and in Russia its title came to stand for the dilemmas of the educated class. Vissarion Belinsky praised it as a masterpiece, and Mirsky thought its prose the best ever written in Russian, though Vladimir Nabokov, who translated it, found the style dry. The 1911 *Encyclopaedia Britannica* called Lermontov "the poet of the Caucasus." Boris Pasternak dedicated his collection *My Sister, Life* (1917) to his memory.

## In this library

- [A Hero of Our Time](hero-of-our-time) — The disillusioned young officer Grigory Pechorin is seen first through the eyes of the old staff captain Maxim Maximych and a travelling narrator, and then through his own journal, which tells of smugglers at Taman, his courtship of Princess Mary and duel with Grushnitsky at a Caucasian spa, and a wager on fate. Its Russian title is *Герой нашего времени*; "Bela" and "The Fatalist" first appeared in *Otechestvennye Zapiski* in 1839 and "Taman" in 1840, and the complete novel was published in two volumes by Ilya Glazunov in St Petersburg in 1840, with a second edition containing the author's preface in 1841.
