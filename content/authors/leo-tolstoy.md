Leo Tolstoy (1828–1910) was a Russian novelist, short-story writer and moral thinker, regarded as one of the greatest authors of all time. He is best known for the novels *War and Peace* and *Anna Karenina*, and in his later years for his Christian teaching of nonviolence and for simple moral tales written for ordinary readers.

## Life

Count Lev Nikolayevich Tolstoy, usually called Leo Tolstoy in English, was born in 1828 at Yasnaya Polyana, his family's estate near Tula, south of Moscow. He was the fourth of five children of Count Nikolai Ilyich Tolstoy, a veteran of the war of 1812, and Princess Mariya Volkonskaya. His mother died when he was two and his father when he was nine, and the children were brought up by relatives. He studied law and oriental languages at Kazan University, where his teachers described him as "both unable and unwilling to learn," and left without finishing his studies. For some years he lived idly at Yasnaya Polyana and in Moscow, Tula and Saint Petersburg.

In 1851, after running up heavy gambling debts, he went with his older brother to the Caucasus and joined the army. There he wrote his first book, *Childhood*, a fictional account of his own early years, published in 1852. As a young artillery officer in the Crimean War he served in Sevastopol during the long siege of 1854–55, was promoted to lieutenant for his courage, and described what he saw in the *Sevastopol Sketches*. He left the army after the war. On journeys through Europe in 1857 and 1860–61 he witnessed a public execution in Paris, which marked him for life, and met the French anarchist Pierre-Joseph Proudhon in Brussels. Back at Yasnaya Polyana, after the emancipation of the serfs in 1861, he founded schools for peasant children, run on the principle that "everything that savours of compulsion is harmful."

In September 1862 Tolstoy married Sophia Andreevna Behrs, a doctor's daughter sixteen years younger than himself. They had thirteen children, eight of whom survived childhood. Sonya acted as his secretary and copied out his manuscripts again and again, and in the early years of the marriage he wrote *War and Peace*, published in full in 1869, and *Anna Karenina*, serialised from 1875 to 1877.

While finishing *Anna Karenina* he fell into a deep moral crisis, which he described in *A Confession*. He broke with the ritual of the Orthodox Church and built a faith on a literal reading of the teachings of Jesus, above all the Sermon on the Mount, which made him a pacifist and a Christian anarchist. He found fellow workers in Vladimir Chertkov and a group of friends who ran the publishing firm Posrednik, and for its readers among the peasants he wrote a number of simple tales directed against common evils such as drunkenness. In 1888 he gave his estates over to his wife and children, and he later renounced the copyrights of his earlier works, which strained his marriage. During the famine of 1891–92 he and his family worked in soup kitchens distributing food and clothing.

His book *The Kingdom of God Is Within You* (1894) set out his doctrine of nonviolent resistance, and his novel *Resurrection* (1899) attacked the courts and the official church; in 1901 the Russian Orthodox Church excommunicated him. In 1909 and 1910 he corresponded with the young Mohandas Gandhi, then an activist in South Africa. In November 1910, acting on a sudden decision to leave Yasnaya Polyana and spend his last days in retirement, he set out by train, fell ill with pneumonia, and died at the railway station of Astapovo at the age of 82.

## Writing

Tolstoy's fiction sets out to portray Russian society realistically, from family life and country estates to Napoleon's headquarters and the battlefields of Austerlitz and Borodino. *War and Peace* has some 580 characters and develops a theory of history in which great individuals matter less than they think; Tolstoy himself did not consider it a novel. Many of his heroes, such as Pierre Bezukhov and Levin, are drawn in his own image. His translator Richard Pevear wrote that his works "are full of provocation and irony, and written with broad and elaborately developed rhetorical devices." Matthew Arnold wrote that "A novel by Tolstoy is not a work of art but a piece of life," and Virginia Woolf called him "the greatest of all novelists."

After his conversion Tolstoy rejected much of his own earlier work, including his two great novels, as "counterfeit art," and turned to writing that he hoped would teach universal brotherly love. His stories for the people are short, plain parables, often built on folk tales and Bible verses. Ludwig Wittgenstein said of "What Men Live By" and several companion tales, "There you have the essence of Christianity!" His ideas on nonviolence influenced Gandhi, who called him "the greatest apostle of non-violence that the present age has produced," and later Martin Luther King Jr. He was nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature every year from 1902 to 1906 but never received it.

## In this library

- [What Men Live By](what-men-live-by) — Simon, a poor shoemaker, takes in a naked stranger found by a roadside chapel, who works for him for years and smiles only three times before revealing himself as an angel sent to learn three truths about human life. It first appeared in a Russian children's magazine in 1881 and was later collected in English as the title story of *What Men Live By, and Other Tales*.
- [Ivan the Fool](ivan-the-fool) — Simple, hard-working Ivan outwits the imps the Devil sends to ruin him and his brothers, Simon the soldier and Taras the merchant, marries a princess and becomes king of a land where only those with work-hardened hands sit down at table. Built on the "lucky fool" of Russian folklore, it was written in the mid-1880s and issued in the Posrednik series of books for the people.
