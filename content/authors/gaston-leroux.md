Gaston Leroux (1868–1927) was a French journalist and novelist, a leading writer of popular detective and adventure fiction in the early twentieth century. He is known for the locked-room mystery *The Mystery of the Yellow Room*, with its young reporter-detective Joseph Rouletabille, and above all for *The Phantom of the Opera*.

## Life

Gaston Louis Alfred Leroux was born in Paris on 6 May 1868; his parents married a month after his birth. He grew up in Normandy, first at Eu and then at Le Tréport, and was educated at the collège in Eu, where Prince Philippe d'Orléans, the young Count of Paris, was also a pupil. Leroux liked to claim a grand pedigree, including descent from William II of England, known in French as Guillaume le Roux. After school he studied law and for a time worked in the law courts.

His way into journalism began with sonnets contributed to *L'Écho de Paris*, for which he also wrote as a court reporter and theatre critic. He then reported on trials for the newspaper *Paris*, covering among others the case of the anarchist bomber Auguste Vaillant, and was recruited by *Le Matin*, where he made his name in 1895 with an interview obtained in the prison cell of an accused man. As a roving special correspondent for *Le Matin* he travelled widely, to Spain and Morocco among other places, and from June 1904 to March 1906 he was its correspondent in Russia, where he reported on the revolution of 1905. He also served the paper as a parliamentary reporter and drama critic.

In 1907 Leroux left journalism for fiction. A play, *La Maison des juges*, staged at the Odéon that year, failed. He then offered ideas for a novel to the weekly *L'Illustration*, which accepted the one built around a young reporter; the result was *Le Mystère de la chambre jaune* (*The Mystery of the Yellow Room*), serialised in *L'Illustration* and published as a book in 1908, which made him famous at once. It was followed by further Rouletabille adventures, beginning with *Le Parfum de la dame en noir* (*The Perfume of the Lady in Black*). *Le Fantôme de l'Opéra* ran in *Le Gaulois* from September 1909 to January 1910 and came out as a book in 1910; an English translation appeared in 1911. *Balaoo* followed in 1911 and was filmed several times. From 1913 he added the series of the convict hero Chéri-Bibi.

Leroux's first marriage, to Marie Lefranc, soon ended in separation. In 1902 he met Jeanne Cayatte, with whom he lived from then on; they had a son and a daughter, Madeleine, and married in 1917. Leroux was made a chevalier of the Legion of Honour. He settled in Nice, where with the actor René Navarre and the writer Arthur Bernède he founded the Société des Cinéromans, a company that issued novels and made them into film serials. Its productions included *Tue-la-Mort*, in which his daughter Madeleine played the lead. The company was later taken over by Pathé. Leroux kept writing novels for the newspapers until the end of his life. He died in Nice on 15 April 1927, at the age of 58, and was buried there in the cemetery of the Château.

## Writing

Leroux wrote fast and for a mass readership, in instalments for the daily and weekly press, and his work mixes the detective story with Gothic horror, adventure and broad comedy. His contribution to French detective fiction has been compared with that of Arthur Conan Doyle in Britain and Edgar Allan Poe in America, both writers he admired. *The Mystery of the Yellow Room*, in which a scientist's daughter is found attacked in a room locked from the inside, is counted among the most celebrated locked-room mysteries; John Dickson Carr had his detective Gideon Fell call it "the best detective tale ever written." Its hero Rouletabille, a young journalist and amateur detective, carried a long series.

*The Phantom of the Opera* draws on the real Palais Garnier and the stories that gathered round it. Leroux used the accident in which a counterweight of the great chandelier fell during a performance, killing a spectator, and the vast water cistern beneath the building, which becomes the Phantom's underground lake. He had been a theatre critic, and he uses music within the novel to prepare its later scenes. The novel was serialised in French, American and English newspapers and became the source of many adaptations, above all the 1925 film with Lon Chaney and Andrew Lloyd Webber's 1986 musical, through which the Phantom is known around the world.
