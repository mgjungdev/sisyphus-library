Jules Verne (1828–1905) was a French novelist, playwright and poet whose long partnership with the publisher Pierre-Jules Hetzel produced the *Voyages extraordinaires*, a series of adventure novels built on the geography and science of his day. He is best known for *Journey to the Centre of the Earth*, *Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas* and *Around the World in Eighty Days*, and he has often been called a "father of science fiction."

## Life

Jules Gabriel Verne was born on 8 February 1828 on the Île Feydeau, then a small island in the Loire within the town of Nantes. His father, Pierre Verne, was an *avoué* (a kind of attorney) from Provins; his mother, Sophie Allotte de La Fuÿe, came from a Nantes family of navigators and shipowners. He was the eldest of five children. At six he was sent to a boarding school whose teacher, the widow of a sea captain, told her pupils that her husband was a castaway who would one day return like Robinson Crusoe, and the theme of the castaway stayed with Verne all his life. He later attended the École Saint-Stanislas, the Petit Séminaire de Saint-Donatien and the Lycée Royal in Nantes, and took the baccalauréat at Rennes in 1846. In his memoir of childhood he recalled a deep fascination with the river and the merchant ships that sailed on it.

His father expected him to inherit the family law practice, and in 1847 sent him to Paris to study law. Verne settled there in July 1848, during the upheavals of the Second Republic. He frequented literary salons, wrote plays under the strong influence of Victor Hugo, and met Alexandre Dumas and his son, Alexandre Dumas fils, who helped him revise a verse comedy, *Les Pailles rompues*, staged at the Théâtre Historique in June 1850. He took his law degree in January 1851, but in January 1852 he refused his father's offer of the Nantes practice and chose a literary life. He worked for several years as secretary of the Théâtre Lyrique and wrote comic operas and libretti. From 1851 the magazine *Musée des familles* published his first stories, among them "A Voyage in a Balloon," which he later described as "the first indication of the line of novel that I was destined to follow." At the Bibliothèque nationale he copied scientific and historical facts onto notecards, a habit he kept for life.

On 10 January 1857 he married Honorine Morel (née du Fraysne de Viane), a widow with two children, and became a stockbroker's associate on the Paris Bourse, writing early each morning before work. Voyages to Scotland in 1858 and to Scandinavia in 1861 were his first journeys abroad; his only son, Michel, was born on 3 August 1861. In 1862 he brought the manuscript of a balloon story to Hetzel, who published it as *Five Weeks in a Balloon* on 31 January 1863 and signed Verne to a long-term contract for three volumes a year. Most of his later novels were serialised in Hetzel's *Magasin d'Éducation et de Récréation* before appearing as books, and in 1866 Hetzel announced that they would form the *Voyages extraordinaires*. The two clashed over *Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas*, when Hetzel refused to let Captain Nemo be a Polish patriot at war with Russia, and Verne afterwards accepted fewer of his publisher's suggestions.

The stage versions of *Around the World in Eighty Days* and *Michel Strogoff*, written with Adolphe d'Ennery, made him wealthy. He bought a series of boats named *Saint-Michel* and sailed around Europe, and he kept homes in Paris and Amiens. He was made a knight of the Legion of Honour in 1870 and an officer in 1892, but was never elected to the Académie française. On 9 March 1886 his nephew Gaston shot him in the leg, leaving him with a permanent limp. After the deaths of Hetzel and of his mother, his books grew darker. In 1888 he was elected to the town council of Amiens, where he served for fifteen years. He died at his home in Amiens on 24 March 1905, suffering from diabetes and the effects of a stroke. Michel Verne saw several more novels through the press, revising them heavily.

## Writing

Verne's method was to join an adventure plot to large amounts of researched fact. Hetzel announced that the series would "outline all the geographical, geological, physical, and astronomical knowledge amassed by modern science," and Verne said late in life: "My object has been to depict the earth, and not the earth alone, but the universe." He insisted that he was not a prophet and that his novels were not meant as science: "I have invented nothing." The robinsonade, the journey across a map, and lonely, driven heroes such as Nemo recur throughout his work.

The 1911 *Encyclopaedia Britannica* praised the "delightfully extravagant voyages and adventures" of his books and their sparkling style, but judged that after 1877 his invention began to fade. George Sand and Théophile Gautier were early admirers, yet his commercial success led many critics, Émile Zola among them, to treat him as a mere genre writer, and Verne said: "The great regret of my life is that I have never taken any place in French literature." From the 1960s French writers and critics such as Roland Barthes and Michel Butor took him seriously as literature, and in 1978 *Journey to the Centre of the Earth* entered the reading list of the French *agrégation*. In English his reputation long suffered from abridged and altered translations marketed to children, which began to give way to fuller translations after 1965. He has been the second most translated author in the world since 1979.

## In this library

- [A Journey to the Centre of the Earth](journey-to-the-centre) — The Hamburg geologist Otto Lidenbrock, his nephew Axel and their Icelandic guide Hans descend through the crater of Snæfellsjökull and find an underground sea and prehistoric life before an eruption of Stromboli throws them back to the surface. It was first published in French by Hetzel in 1864 and reissued in a revised and expanded edition in 1867.
- [Twenty Thousand Leagues under the Sea](twenty-thousand-leagues) — The naturalist Pierre Aronnax, his servant Conseil and the harpooner Ned Land are taken aboard Captain Nemo's submarine *Nautilus* and carried through the oceans of the world. It was serialised in Hetzel's *Magasin d'Éducation et de Récréation* from March 1869 to June 1870.
- [Around the World in Eighty Days](around-the-world) — Phileas Fogg of the Reform Club wagers £20,000 that he can circle the globe in eighty days and sets out from London with his new French valet, Passepartout, pursued by Detective Fix. It was serialised in *Le Temps* from 6 November 1872 and published as a book in 1873.
